# Body Shape

Design notes for cutting the body outline.

Concept sketch: [`brainstorm/guitar-concept.svg`](../brainstorm/guitar-concept.svg).

## Direction

**Jazzmaster × Iceman hybrid** — roughly a **flipped (reverse-offset)
Jazzmaster** with a **larger lower horn** (Iceman influence) and **soft curves
all around**, pushed toward modern/heavier sensibilities. Comfortable offset
shape as the base; Iceman lower-horn sweep for attitude; nothing pointy.

- **"Flipped" = reverse offset** — mirror the offset so the larger bout/horn
  swaps sides. Modern look. _Re-run the balance check on the mirrored outline_
  (see §1) — upper-horn reach that balances a normal Jazzmaster may not carry
  over when flipped.
- **Soft curves throughout** — rounded horns are short-grain-tolerant and far
  easier to sand/finish in open-grained korina. This sidesteps the fragile-
  sharp-horn problem (see §4); keep it that way.

## Starting point

- Body is a **rectangular, pre-routed korina blank** — full freedom to cut the
  outer shape.
- **Some features are already cut/drilled and positioned for 25.5" scale.** The
  outline is drawn _around_ these fixed reference points — they can't move:
  - **Filtertron-sized route** at neck (analog pickup) — pre-routed. Ordered as
    a TV Jones route; the Porter that was actually bought is the same size.
  - **Neck pocket** — pre-routed
  - **Bridge mounting holes** — pre-drilled → fixes bridge position and the
    string path/centerline
- **Not pre-cut (you do it later):**
  - **Rear control cavity** — omitted from the order (no upcharge); route it
    yourself after the outline is drawn so it fits the final control layout
  - **The bridge route** for the 6 hex pickups —
    cut after measuring string spacing off the installed bridge. Leave that area
    open and don't let the lower horn / contours encroach on it.
- **Locate and mark the pre-cut routes, the bridge holes, and the resulting
  string centerline first**, then draw the outline to fit them. The drilled
  bridge holes + neck pocket pin the whole layout — work outward from them.
  Don't draw the shape first.

## Constraints & considerations

### 1. Balance / neck dive — the #1 driver

The neck is **dense purpleheart with 24 frets**, pushing mass and length toward
the headstock → real neck-dive risk.

- The fix for neck dive is a **long _upper_ horn** carrying the rear strap
  button back toward the **12th fret** (SG-like reach).
- The Iceman's signature **extended _lower_ horn adds drama but does nothing for
  balance** (wrong end). So: let the lower horn be the showpiece, but let
  **balance dictate the upper horn** — make it long enough, or move the rear
  strap button onto the neck-heel side.
- **Test balance before the final cut**: mock the outline in cardboard/MDF,
  mount the actual neck + bridge + tuners, hang it on a strap, and check where
  it wants to sit. Adjust the upper horn / strap-button position accordingly.

### 2. Ergonomics — keep the offset waist

- The Jazzmaster's **offset waist** is what makes it sit so well seated. Keep it.
- The Iceman is less comfortable seated (the lower point digs into the leg).
  Hybrid plan: **Jazzmaster offset waist + comfort, Iceman lower-horn sweep for
  looks.**
- Consider a belly contour (back) and forearm contour (front) for comfort.

### 3. Control real estate

- Lots to place: **6 toggle switches, 3 knobs, 13-pin DIN jack**, plus the rear
  cavity. The 13-pin GK socket is physically larger than a 1/4" jack — check
  its panel cutout and depth against the cavity before committing the outline.
- A larger lower bout (which the Iceman direction gives) helps here — just make
  sure the swept lower horn doesn't eat into where the controls and rear cavity
  need to live. Mock up the control layout on the outline before cutting.

### 4. Buildability — korina + the soft-curve plan

- The **soft-curves-all-around** direction is the buildability-friendly choice:
  rounded horns are short-grain-tolerant and don't chip the way needle points
  do. Keep it that way — resist the urge to sharpen the lower horn tip.
- **Korina is open-grained**; under a transparent finish, any tearout/chipping
  shows. Rounded **inside** transitions are far easier to sand and finish
  cleanly than sharp inside corners — keep all inside transitions radiused.
- Even soft horns: go slow sanding the convex curves so you don't round them
  over unevenly by hand.

## Process checklist

1. Mark the pre-cut routes, the **pre-drilled bridge holes**, the neck pocket,
   and the resulting string centerline on the blank.
2. Draw candidate outline(s) around them — paper or cardboard template first.
   Leave the future hex-pickup bridge route area clear.
3. Cardboard/MDF mock-up → mount neck + bridge + tuners → strap test for
   balance (test the **flipped/reverse-offset** outline specifically).
4. Adjust upper horn / strap-button position until it hangs neutral.
5. Confirm control layout fits the lower bout + rear cavity.
6. Transfer template to the blank, cut, then radius edges — keep all curves
   soft, round inside transitions.
